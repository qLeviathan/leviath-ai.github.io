About the Founder

A journey from quantum physics to financial markets to recursive AI architecture

My path began at UIUC studying quantum chemistry, thermodynamics, and electricity & magnetism (my favorite class ever). I dove deep into atmospheric physics, fluid dynamics, vector field calculus, and even researched the emergence of thunder in horizontal snowstorms. No degree—just pure learning driven by curiosity.

The quantum well of options trading taught me that money dissipates faster than market selloffs, but more importantly, that quantum superposition mirrors price emergence from supply and demand. Economics became simpler than physics—just logarithmic transforms of multidimensional interactions.

At DePaul University, game theory clicked immediately. Econometrics revealed itself as linear explanations of complex field theory. Beta coefficients? Measurements of entanglement strength. Panel logit hazard modeling? Basically trigonometry—topologically constrained by error terms and rotational mechanisms.

During trading, I used pure visual price dynamics with Fibonacci levels, understanding boundary conditions as magnetic flux conditions. The sequence of information matters— exogenous factors determine price movement permissions.

Today, I build AI systems on quantum field principles, where first and second-order terms coexist linearly, tokens collapse via Born rule mechanics, and binary models sequence positioning topologically. We're in the early phases of AGI+ testing with clear mathematical frameworks.
